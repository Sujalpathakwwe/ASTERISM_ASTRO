THE NORTH NODE

Rahu

✦

Desire, Ambition & the Unknown

In Vedic astrology, Rahu is traditionally associated with intense desire, ambition, unconventional experiences and the urge to move beyond familiar boundaries.

Unlike the visible planets, Rahu is a lunar node. Its symbolism concerns what attracts attention, creates fascination and pushes a person toward experiences that may feel new, unusual or difficult to ignore.

ASTROLOGICAL PERSPECTIVES

Understanding Rahu

01

Rahu in Vedic Astrology

In Vedic astrology, Rahu is the north lunar node and is traditionally interpreted as a powerful force of desire, worldly experience, ambition and fascination.

It can indicate areas where a person wants more, wants faster results or feels compelled to experience something outside their usual boundaries.

Rahu can produce both extraordinary growth and confusion. Its expression depends heavily on its sign, house, aspects, conjunctions, dispositor and the wider birth chart.

02

Rahu in Western Astrology

In Western astrology, the equivalent point is generally called the North Node. It is often interpreted as a direction of development, unfamiliar experience and personal growth.

The emphasis is less on a physical planet and more on the symbolic direction toward which life appears to pull the individual.

BEYOND ASTROLOGY

Rahu in Other Traditions

✦

Rahu & Numerology

Some numerological traditions associate Rahu with number 4, particularly within systems influenced by Indian numerological practice.

The symbolism can include unconventional thinking, disruption, ambition, experimentation and a tendency to challenge established patterns.

Numerology and astrology are separate interpretive systems, and correspondences vary between traditions.
✦

Rahu in Palmistry

Rahu does not have a universally standardised physical representation in classical palmistry in the same way that the traditional planetary mounts do.

Some modern Indian palmistry traditions use Rahu-related interpretations for unusual markings, disruptions or areas connected with sudden changes.

Palmistry traditions vary considerably, so these interpretations should not be treated as universal.
